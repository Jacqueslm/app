# The Day One Plan

**Named 12 Aug 2026 by Jacques.** Not "the 2pm plan" — **the Day One Plan**,
because it carries the app's name and because the thing it is actually for is
getting somebody back to day one.

**Status: specification, not built.** The rule stands — no new app features
until through the Play Store. This exists so the thinking survives the
conversation it came out of.

---

## Why this needs a name at all

Across every AI-search lane, the biggest topics are **named things**, not
questions: Rethinking Drinking (7,300), the Betrayal Bond (3,100), Allen Carr's
Easy Way (1,500), Kick the Drink (1,300), Brain Over Binge (258).

When somebody asks an AI how to stop, they ask for **a method they
half-remember the name of**. The app has a name. The method inside it didn't —
so nobody could ask for it, search it, or repeat it to a friend.

---

## The correction that makes this different from every other app

The original spec was half a plan: *write it at 2pm, for the 2am version of
you.* Prevention. Every recovery app on the market has some version of that.

**Jacques' correction, 12 Aug, in his words:**

> "if you relapse — because the hardest part is after a relapse. it's easy to
> start after you satisfied the urge, but it's hard to come back after you're
> falling. that's the part where most people fail. it's when you become
> hopeless, reject yourself, think you are a monster, hate yourself, want to
> hurt yourself."

That is the whole product insight, and it is lived, not researched.

**The counter-intuitive part, which is why nobody builds it:** the moment right
*after* using is calm. The urge is answered. You can think, you can plan, you
can promise. Starting is easy there. **The hard part is the day after** — when
the calm is gone and what's left is the shame, and the person who has to come
back is the person who now believes he's a monster.

Every app builds for the craving. **Almost nobody builds for the morning after
the fall.** That is where people actually leave.

So the Day One Plan has two halves, and the second is the one that matters.

---

## HALF ONE — Before

Written when you're fine, for when you're not.

Four prompts, no more. Written once, editable anytime, shown in full when the
panic button is pressed.

1. **What it feels like just before.** In your own words. Not a symptom list —
   the actual tell. "I start checking if anyone's home."
2. **What I'm going to do instead, for ten minutes.** One thing. Physical,
   specific, available at 3am.
3. **Who I'm telling.** One name, one number. Pre-filled so it's a tap, not a
   decision.
4. **What I lose if I don't.** In your handwriting, not a motivational line.
   The specific thing.

---

## HALF TWO — After

**This is the half nobody else has.** Written before you fall, read only after.

It stays locked until you mark a relapse — it should not be sitting there as a
permission slip. What it opens with is not advice. It is you, talking to
yourself, from before.

Five prompts:

1. **"Read this first."** One line you write to yourself for this exact moment.
   The only thing on the screen at first — everything else is a tap further.
2. **What I know about myself that I won't believe right now.** Written when you
   still believed it.
3. **How long the shame usually lasts.** Naming a duration is what makes it
   survivable — a feeling with an end is a different thing from a verdict.
4. **What coming back looked like last time.** Not what going out looked like.
5. **The first small thing I do.** Not "get sober again." Something today-sized.
   Eat. Shower. Open the app.

**The counter is the point.** When a relapse is marked, the app must not read
as punishment. The number going back to zero is a fact, not a judgement, and
the wording around it decides whether somebody opens the app again or deletes
it. **Streaks reset. The person doesn't.** Nothing in this flow says "failed",
"broken", "start over", or "again" in a tone that lands as *again?*

---

## The safety step — not optional

Jacques named the real end of that spiral: *want to hurt yourself.* That is
common after a relapse and it is the reason this half exists at all.

**Rules for the After flow:**

- If a person indicates they're thinking about hurting themselves, the flow
  **stops being a plan and becomes one screen**: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
  — call or text, free, confidential, 24 hours. 988 already appears across the
  site; it belongs here first.
- **No streak talk, no stats, no upsell on that screen.** Nothing to dismiss
  before the number is visible.
- The app never diagnoses and never says a person is in danger. It puts the
  number in front of them and gets out of the way.
- This screen is reachable at any point in the After flow, not only if
  triggered.

---

## Why this is the moat, in one line

Every competitor helps you *not start*. **This is the only one that helps you
come back** — written by someone who fell for 38 years and did come back at 50.

## What to do with it, and when

**Not now.** Play Store first.

But the name is usable immediately, and should be, everywhere:
- **Script 17** in `LAUNCH-SCRIPTS.md` — retitle to "The Day One Plan"
- The site, when the pages next get touched
- Any AI-search page — this is the term people would half-remember

**And it is a video, probably the best one on the list.** Nobody makes content
about the morning after. Everyone makes content about the craving. A 15-second
talking head that opens *"The hard part isn't the night you drink. It's the
morning after"* has no competition in the whole keyword file.
