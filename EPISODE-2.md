## What changed from Manus's draft, and why

**The title.** "The First Step" describes a self-help chapter. The episode's
own best line is *"Day one was surviving the night. Day two is surviving the
afternoon."* That's the whole idea, so it's the title now.

**It ends on the notebook, not a window.** Episode 1 finished on Elias at a
window in morning light with a glass of water. Manus's Episode 2 finished on
Elias at a window in morning light with a glass of water. Same shot, different
room. A second episode that ends where the first one ended doesn't feel like a
series, it feels like a loop. The notebook — "Day 2" in his own handwriting —
is the ending nobody has seen.

**The walk is gone.** Stepping outside, walking, and passing the corner store
was 22 seconds of a man walking. Stepping out and the corner store are beats;
the walk between them is connective tissue. Cut.

**The afternoon is new, and it's the point.** The script says day two is about
surviving the afternoon and then never shows an afternoon — it goes from a
morning walk straight to writing in a notebook. So there's now a shot of the
hour this episode is named for: three o'clock, nothing to do, nobody watching,
the television off. That's the hard part, and every draft of this skipped it.

**"It doesn't get easier. You just get stronger" is gone.** It's on a million
gym posters. Everything else here sounds like a person.

**The words on screen are first person.** Manus wrote captions as labels — "The
harsh light of day", "The familiar pull". Episode 1 landed because the words
were somebody talking: *"I wasn't hungry. I was filling a hole."*

**46 seconds, 8 shots.** Down from 67. Episode 1 was 28.

**Two panels already exist** — Scenes 1 and 2. Six need making, including a
new Shot 3 so the phone shows your app.

**This is where your app goes, not Shot 2.** Shot 2 has to stay the delivery
app, because it's the thing pulling him — if the phone shows your app at the
moment of the craving, there's nothing for him to resist and the beat
collapses. One shot later is where it earns its place: he closes the thing
that was pulling him and opens the thing that helps, and the screen you see is
the SOS breathing circle actually doing its job. That's the app working, in
the moment it's for. A logo on a table is an advert; this is a demonstration.

**Why those moves.** Zoom in twice at the start — the morning closing in, then
the pull. Hover on the breath, because it holds and goes nowhere. 3D depth
when he steps out, so the world opens up in front of him. Looking up at the
corner store, so the shop is bigger than he is. Zoom out on the return, to
show him the same four walls. Looking down on three o'clock, so he's small in
an empty room. Zoom in on the pen — the only thing in the episode worth moving
closer to.

## Suno prompt — instrumental, like Episode 1

Turn the **Instrumental** switch on so it can't sing over the voiceover.

```
Slow sparse instrumental score. Felt piano, single notes with silence between
them, never hurrying toward a melody. One low sustained cello underneath that
never quite resolves. Faint tape hiss and room tone left in. 62 bpm, minor,
unhurried. No drums for the first half; from the midpoint a soft heartbeat
pulse felt more than heard, and a high sustained string that rises and never
arrives anywhere. No swell, no drop, no climax. It should end more settled
than it started, not more triumphant. Lots of empty space in the mix for
speech and room sound to sit in. Restrained, patient, documentary.
```

**Why it's built like that.** Every sound cue in this episode is small and
specific — one breath, a neon hum, a pen on paper, a fridge two rooms away. A
score with a big middle would bury all of it. And the episode isn't a victory,
it's an ordinary Tuesday survived, so a track that climaxes would be telling
the viewer something the pictures aren't.

**Using it:** take the quietest 46 seconds of whatever Suno returns, not the
first 46. Under **Shot 7, three o'clock, fade the music out entirely** and let
the fridge hum carry it. That silence is the loudest moment in the episode and
music will soften it.

### Two other directions if that one comes back wrong

- **Colder:** `Warm analogue synth drone, no percussion at all, slowly shifting
  chords, tape saturation, one note held far too long. Ambient, isolating,
  patient. No melody.` — makes the afternoon lonelier and the room bigger.
- **More human:** `Fingerpicked acoustic guitar, close-mic'd with string
  noise and breath audible, one repeating figure that never develops, no
  percussion, no build.` — warmer, closer to a person, less like a film score.
