## What you shoot yourself — the three notebook frames

Ten minutes, a pen, a plain lined notebook and a phone. No app, no generator.

Sit at a wooden table. One lamp on, everything else off, late evening. Shoot
from directly above or just over your own shoulder, portrait, and get close.

**SHOT 1 — TWELVE DAYS.** Write a column down the page, one line each: *Day 1*
through *Day 12*. Short entries after some of them if you want — two or three
words, not sentences. Leave the pen lying beside it. Photograph it in warm
afternoon light, wide enough to see the whole column.

**SHOT 6 — HOME.** Same notebook, next page. Your hand mid-stroke writing
**Day 13**. Late at night, one lamp, the rest of the room dark. Pen actually
touching the paper.

**SHOT 7 — THE PAGES.** Your hand turning back through the filled pages —
page after page of the same handwriting. This is the shot the episode turns on,
so if the earlier pages look cramped and the later ones look looser, that is
the whole story told without a word.

**Yes, it will be your hands and not Elias's.** That is not a problem — it is
the point. Episode 3 ends with you on camera saying it was never a story. Hands
that stop being an actor's a shot early is the seam starting to show, which is
exactly what shot 8 does on purpose.

## Why not all seven from Manus

Three of the seven are handwriting, and handwriting is the one thing that came
back wrong in Episode 2. "Day 2" — two characters — arrived legible in one
version and **upside down** in another. Shot 1 here needs twelve lines legible
and in order. Shot 7 needs a whole notebook of consistent handwriting that
visibly steadies over time.

That is not a prompt problem, it is what image generators are bad at. Asking
harder is not the fix; not asking is. A real notebook photographed on a phone
will beat a generated one every time, costs nothing, and can be reshot in
thirty seconds if the light is wrong.

The four Manus shots are the four it is genuinely good at: a lit phone from
above, a crowded room, a close-up hand, a person alone under a streetlight.

## Check these when the four come back

**Shot 2 — is he actually separate?** If he reads as part of the group, ask
again: nobody at the table looking at him, and only water in front of him.

**Shot 3 — is the hand tense?** A relaxed hand is a different shot. It should
look like it is holding the table down.

**Shot 4 — is there space above his head?** If it comes back tight on him, ask
for a wider frame. The emptiness is the shot.

**Any second figure?** Episode 2 nearly produced two Eliases off one word about
a reflection. If a second person appears on the pavement in shot 4, regenerate.
