# Starter pack: the first $505, the first 78 units

Read `HERBS-BUSINESS.md` first. This file is the shopping cart, the recipes, the
label copy, and the price sheet — the part that gets you to a real table in four
weeks.

Prices are the 2026 wholesale snapshots from that document. Where I had no
supplier price (pouches, carrier oil, wax) it is marked **est.** and you must

## The product line, chosen to match what you already teach

Your `herbs.html` app covers ginger, chamomile, turmeric, nettle, lavender,
mullein, yarrow. The line below uses those, in the order that is cheapest to
legalise:

| # | Product | Category | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Calendula salve, 2 oz** | Cosmetic | No kitchen licence in most states. Best margin per unit. |
| 2 | **Elderberry tincture, 1 oz** | Supplement | Highest price per ounce. Needs a Supplement Facts panel. |
| 3 | **Nettle & peppermint tea, 4 oz** | Food | Cheapest to legalise, the entry-price item that gets a stranger to your table. |

**Deliberately not in batch one:** capsules (GMP, testing, Amazon documentation),
anything with a proprietary blend, anything with a disease claim, and anything
herbal in a bottle below 0.5% ABV labelled "tincture" without checking TTB.

Materials per unit against a realistic retail price:

| Product | Materials | Retail | Contribution | Wholesale | Contribution at wholesale |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Calendula salve 2 oz | **$2.64** | $18 | **$15.36** | $9 | **$6.36** |
| Elderberry tincture 1 oz | **$2.10** | $16 | **$13.90** | $8 | **$5.90** |
| Nettle & mint tea 4 oz | **$6.49** | $14 | **$7.51** | $7 | **$0.51** |

Two conclusions, and they should shape everything:

1. **Tea is a marketing product, not a profit product.** $6.49 of materials in a
   $14 bag. It exists to be the $14 thing someone buys before they trust you
   with the $18 salve.
2. **Never wholesale the tea.** At keystone you'd clear 51 cents a bag. Wholesale
   only the salve and the tincture, where the shop still leaves you ~$6.

### The three batches

**Calendula salve — 24 × 2 oz**
- 38 fl oz olive oil + 4.8 oz dried calendula, infuse 3–4 weeks (or 3 h in a
  double boiler at 65–70 °C), strain through muslin.
- Melt 9.6 oz beeswax into the oil, stir, pour at 60 °C.
- Materials: herb $11.20 + oil $13 + wax $6 + jar $28.56 + label $4.80 = **$63.56**,
  or **$2.65 each**.

**Elderberry tincture — 30 × 1 oz**
- 6 oz dried elderberry in 12 fl oz of 190-proof, 4–6 weeks, shake daily.
- Press, filter, cut with 18 fl oz water to ~40% ABV, rest 1 week.
- Materials: herb $12.01 + alcohol $8.40 + bottles $36.60 + labels $6.00 =
  **$63.01**, or **$2.10 each**. Add a printed carton and it is $2.35, which is
  the figure to use if you sell to shops. (Yields are lossy — expect 3–5% short.)

**Nettle & peppermint tea — 24 × 4 oz**
- 70/30 blend: 67.2 oz nettle + 28.8 oz peppermint (4.2 lb + 1.8 lb).
- Materials: $101.55 + $39.37 + pouches $10 + labels $4.80 = **$155.72**, or
  **$6.49 each**.

## Label copy you can actually print

### Calendula salve (cosmetic — no Supplement Facts)

> **CALENDULA SALVE**
> 2 oz (56 g)
>
> Ingredients: extra virgin olive oil infused with *Calendula officinalis*
> flowers, beeswax.
> Directions: apply to dry skin as needed. For external use only. Discontinue if
> irritation occurs.
> [Your name or LLC], [City, State] · Lot 001 · Best by [date]

**Never print:** heals, treats, cures, eczema, rash, infection, inflammation,
scar. Any of those makes it a drug.

### Nettle & peppermint tea (food)

> **NETTLE & PEPPERMINT**
> Herbal tea · 4 oz (113 g)
>
> Ingredients: stinging nettle leaf (*Urtica dioica*), peppermint leaf
> (*Mentha × piperita*).
> Brew: 1 tsp per cup, 5–7 minutes. Store cool and dry.
> [Your name or LLC], [City, State] · Net wt 4 oz

A small-business Nutrition Facts exemption likely applies at your volume, but
only if you make **no** nutrition or health claims on the package or in
advertising. Don't claim "rich in iron" on a label you haven't panel'd.

### Elderberry tincture (supplement)

> **ELDERBERRY TINCTURE**
> 1 fl oz (30 ml) · 40% alcohol by volume
>
> **Supplement Facts** — Serving size 1 ml (about 30 drops) · Servings per
> container about 30 · Elderberry (*Sambucus nigra*) fruit extract ___ mg ·
> *Daily Value not established.*
>
> Other ingredients: organic cane alcohol, purified water.
> Directions: 1 dropperful in water up to 3× daily. Shake well.
> Store away from heat and light.
> [Your name or LLC], [City, State] · Lot 001 · Best by [date]

If you put any benefit on the front — "supports immune function" is the usual
one — you must also print:

> This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration.
> This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

…and notify FDA within 30 days of first marketing. If you'd rather not, print
the product name and directions only.

## The three mistakes that cost the most

1. **Buying 5 lb of an herb you've never made with.** The ¼ lb tier exists for a
   reason; taste and test before you commit $100.
2. **Printing 300 labels before you've held the jar.** Label size, curve and
   adhesive are the most common $85 mistake.
3. **Selling the tincture before the insurance is active.** It is the product
   with the highest claim exposure and the lowest tolerance for being wrong.
