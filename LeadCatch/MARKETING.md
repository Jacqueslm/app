## Read this first: two things block charging money

**No password reset.** A customer who forgets their password is stuck until you
fix it by hand in the database. With ten customers that's an annoyance. With a
hundred it's your whole week. Fix before you push hard.

**No billing.** Nothing charges anyone.

The second one isn't actually blocking, and don't let it stall you: **launch
free, explicitly as a beta.** "Free while I'm building it, and you keep that
price for a year if you're one of the first fifty" is a better cold-email offer
than any discount, and it buys you the thing you actually need right now, which
is twenty businesses using it and telling you what's broken. Add Stripe when
people are annoyed you haven't taken their money.

Password reset, though — that one needs doing first. It's an afternoon.

## The positioning: don't sell a form

Nobody wants a contact form. Wix, Squarespace, Jotform, Google Forms and
HubSpot all give one away, and you will lose a feature fight with every one of
them.

What you sell is **speed**. The number that matters in home services is
time-to-first-contact: the business that calls back first usually wins the job,
and most small businesses find out about an enquiry hours later, if the form
works at all. LeadCatch emails them the phone number in seconds with the message
in the preview pane.

So the pitch is never "a better form." It's:

> **"You're losing jobs to whoever calls back first. Right now that isn't you."**

`speed to lead` is a real term with 1,300 searches a month and only KD 27 — it's
a phrase the market already uses. Build everything on it.

**Pick one trade and only one.** Plumbers, electricians, roofers, HVAC. Reasons:
a job is worth $200–$5,000, so the ROI sentence writes itself — *one extra job
pays for a year* — and they're rarely at a desk, which is exactly the problem
you solve. `plumber marketing` runs a $25.95 CPC, which tells you what these
customers are worth to everyone else selling to them.

Sell to plumbers until it's boring. Then do electricians.

## Channel 1 — build it for them before they say yes

This is the whole strategy at zero customers, and LeadCatch has an unfair
advantage most tools don't: the hosted form page (`/f/KEY`). **You can build a
working, branded form for a business that has never heard of you and send them
a live link to it.** No signup, no demo call, nothing to install.

The loop, about 4 minutes each:

1. Google Maps → "plumber in [your city]". Open the website of each result.
2. Look for one of three tells:
   - no contact form at all, just a phone number
   - a form that's plainly abandoned (Wix/Squarespace default, no reply promise)
   - a form you can submit that gives no confirmation of anything
3. **Submit their form with your own details** and a real message: *"Hi — do you
   do emergency callouts in [area]? — [your name], [your number]"*. Note the time.
4. In LeadCatch, make a form named for their business, their headline, their
   colour. Grab the `/f/KEY` link.
5. Wait for them to reply to step 3. Most won't, or will take days. **That silence
   is your entire pitch.**

Then send this:

> **Subject:** your website contact form
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I filled in the contact form on [theirsite.com] on Tuesday morning asking
> about an emergency callout. It's Thursday and I haven't heard back — I'm not
> chasing a job, I just wanted to check whether it's reaching you.
>
> If it isn't, that's money walking. I build a tool that emails you the person's
> name and number about ten seconds after they hit send, so you can ring them
> back while they're still on your website instead of after they've called
> someone else.
>
> I made you one already, it's live right now: [your /f/KEY link]
>
> Try it on your phone. If you like it, it's one line of code on your site and
> I'll paste it in for you. Free while I'm building it out.
>
> [Your name]
> [your number]

Why it works: it isn't a pitch, it's a bug report about their business, with
evidence, and the fix already built. Almost nobody cold-emails with the product
already made.

**If they replied to your test in under an hour, leave them alone.** They've got
this handled and you'll waste both your time. Move to the next one.

Do ten a day. Ten replies out of a hundred sends is a good week, and three
installs out of that is a real start.

## Channel 2 — web designers are the multiplier

One freelancer who builds sites for local businesses installs you on twenty
sites. This is by far your highest-leverage channel and almost nobody's
competing for it, because everyone chases the end business.

Find them: Google "web designer [your city]", local Facebook business groups,
the credit link in the footer of every small-business site you looked at in
Channel 1 (*"Site by ___"* — that person built ten more just like it).

> **Subject:** contact forms for your client sites
>
> Hi [name] — I saw you built [clientsite.com].
>
> I've made a contact-form tool aimed at trades: one script tag, the client gets
> the enquiry emailed in seconds, and there's a dashboard where they mark who
> they've called back. It doesn't need a plugin or a page builder.
>
> The bit that might matter to you: the client stops emailing *you* when their
> form breaks. They get their own login.
>
> Free accounts for anything you install while I'm in beta. Want a link to try
> on one site?
>
> [Your name]

## Channel 3 — the alternative pages, again

You already have this machine — you built two dozen `*-alternative.html` pages
for the recovery app. Point it at this. The competitors here are far softer than
the ones in recovery:

| `typeform-alternative` | 320 | 19 | Typeform is expensive — lead with price |
| `speed-to-lead` | 1,300 | 27 | Your positioning term. Make it the pillar page. |
| `missed-call-text-back` | 390 | 18 | Adjacent category trades already search for |
| `lead-capture-form` | 480 | 34 | $14.60 CPC — buyers, not browsers |
| `plumber-marketing` | 1,600 | 22 | $25.95 CPC. Write the trade guide, embed a demo form. |
| `lead-management-software-for-small-business` | 170 | 46 | Small but $28.41 CPC |

Skip `small business crm` (2,900/mo but KD 69) and `free crm for small business`
(1,900, KD 57). Those SERPs belong to HubSpot and Zoho and you will not move
them.

Angle for each alternative page: not "we have more features" — you don't. It's
*"Jotform is a form builder. This is a form that phones you."* Free tier, no
per-response limits, built for one trade rather than everybody.

## What to charge, when you charge

$19/month, one plan, unlimited forms and leads. Reasons: it's under the threshold
where a tradesperson needs to think about it, one recovered job pays for the year
several times over, and a single price means no comparison table and no upgrade
nag. Founding fifty keep it at $19 forever — say so in writing, then honour it.

Don't do a free tier at launch. Do a 30-day trial with no card. Free tiers fill
your database with people who will never pay and whose spam you'll be filtering
for free.

## Do this week

1. Build password reset. Half a day, and it's the one thing that will hurt.
2. Deploy it somewhere with a real domain and set `RESEND_API_KEY` — the whole
   pitch is the email, so the email has to actually arrive.
3. Run Channel 1 ten times a day, five days. That's fifty tests and fifty emails.
4. Write `jotform-alternative`. KD 9 will not stay KD 9 forever.
5. When you get your first three customers, ask each one the same question:
   *what nearly stopped you signing up?* Fix that, before building anything new.

The first ten customers come from your own hands, one at a time. Nothing on this
page scales until that's done, and trying to skip it is the most common way this
kind of product dies with a beautiful landing page and no users.
