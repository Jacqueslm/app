What is hangxiety?

It's the anxiety that arrives with a hangover — often worse than the headache, and for a lot of people it's the only part that really hurts. Dread with no subject. A conviction you've done something wrong that survives all evidence you haven't.

It's not a personality flaw and it's not you being dramatic. It's chemistry, and it's extremely common.

Alcohol is a depressant. While you're drinking it turns the volume down — that's the loose, easy feeling. Your brain doesn't like being turned down, so it pushes back the other way to stay level. When the drink wears off, the push-back is still running with nothing to push against. You're left over-revved. That's the 4am.

What does hangxiety feel like?

Most people describe some mix of these:

  • Waking early — 3, 4, 5am — with your heart going before you're properly awake
  • Certainty you embarrassed yourself, with no actual memory of doing so
  • Re-reading your messages. Then re-reading them again
  • Shame that's out of all proportion to the night
  • Restlessness that makes sitting still feel impossible, and a body too wrecked to move
  • The private promise: never again, this time I mean it

If several of those are familiar, you're not unusual. You're describing the standard experience.

How long does hangxiety last?

Usually it lifts with the hangover — most of a day, gone by evening or once you've slept properly. Some people get a second, thinner day of it.

The thing to know: it does end. Every time. Nothing you do in the middle of it is speeding that up much.

If the low is still there after two or three days, or if it's there on days you didn't drink at all, that's worth taking to a doctor — not because something's wrong with you, but because that's a different thing wearing the same clothes.

What actually helps

Nothing fixes it outright. These make the hours pass better:

  • Water and food. Dull, and it genuinely matters. Low blood sugar makes anxiety worse, and you're dehydrated.
  • Don't lie there. Get up, get outside, get daylight. Ten minutes of walking does more than an hour of lying still replaying it.
  • Check the story. The dread will insist you did something. Ask what, specifically. It rarely has an answer.
  • Don't apologize to everyone. The urge to send five clearing-the-air messages is the anxiety, not good manners. Wait until evening. It'll have gone.
  • No hair of the dog. It works for twenty minutes and hands the whole thing back with interest.
  • Say it out loud to one person. Hangxiety survives on being a secret. It shrinks fast when named.

And the plain truth nobody wants: the only thing that prevents it is drinking less, or not at all. Everything above is a way through it, not a way around it.

When it's every weekend

Once is a night out. Every weekend is a pattern, and the pattern is telling you something you already suspect — which is why you're reading this at whatever time it is.

You don't need a rock bottom to decide you've had enough of the mornings.

Most people don't stop because something terrible happened. They stop because they got tired of the 4am. That's a good enough reason. It's the reason I stopped, at 50, after 38 years of them.
