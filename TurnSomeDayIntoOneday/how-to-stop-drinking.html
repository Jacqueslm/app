How to stop drinking

You don't need a rock bottom. Tired is enough.

Most people searching this haven't crashed a car or lost a job. They're just tired — of the 3 a.m. wake-up, of doing the math on the recycling, of promising Sunday-morning-you something Friday-night-you keeps taking back. You don't have to earn the right to stop by falling further first.

Read this first: stopping suddenly can be dangerous

One thing before anything else, because it's the one thing on this page that isn't about willpower: if you've been drinking heavily every day for a long time, stopping cold can be genuinely dangerous for your body — this is the one addiction where quitting abruptly can hurt you. That's a doctor conversation, and it's a short one: "I drink this much, I want to stop, how do I do it safely?" Doctors have this conversation every week. Have it before day one, not after something scares you.

The evening is the real opponent

Nobody relapses at 10 a.m. The fight is a window — for most people somewhere between the end of work and the first hour of dark, when the day's weight lands and the old answer is in the fridge. Thirty-eight years of drinking taught me the window is survivable, but only if you stop treating it as a test of character and start treating it like weather: it arrives, it peaks, it passes, every single night, whether you drink or not.

You don't have to be strong for a day. You have to get through one window, tonight.

That's why "one day at a time" survived every fashion in recovery — it's the actual size of the fight. A day. Sometimes ten minutes of one.

On your own — the honest version

Plenty of people quit without meetings. If rooms full of folding chairs aren't for you, that doesn't disqualify you from stopping. But "on your own" can't mean "in secret from everyone, with no plan, relying on wanting it badly enough." That version has a track record, and you've lived it.

On your own works when it means: your own counter, so the days become something you'd hate to lose. Your own plan for the window. Something to do in the worst ten minutes that isn't arguing with a fridge. And somewhere honest to put what the drinking was actually doing for you — because it was doing something, and that something still needs an answer sober.
