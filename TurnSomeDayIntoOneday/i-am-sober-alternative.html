Looking for an I Am Sober alternative?

Updated August 2026

Full disclosure: this page lives on the site of one of the apps compared — ours. We'll still be straight with you about both, because different apps genuinely fit different people, and the fastest way to lose your trust would be to pretend otherwise.

If you're reading this, you've probably already tried I Am Sober, or you're deciding between it and something else. Fair. It's the most downloaded app in this space, and it got there honestly. Here's a straight look at where it shines and where you might want something built differently.

What I Am Sober does well

I Am Sober

The best-known sobriety tracker there is

The day counter is clean and satisfying. The morning pledge and evening review give your day a shape — a small ritual that a lot of people genuinely stick with. The community is one of the largest anywhere: whatever milestone you hit, someone else hit it today too, and you can see their faces and stories.

It also covers many habits, not just alcohol, and the free version is enough for plenty of people to get started.

Free basics · paid tier — check their site for current pricing

Where it may not fit

I Am Sober is built for one person: the one doing the quitting. That's not a flaw — it's just its shape. If your spouse or partner is going through this with you, there's nothing in it for them to open.

It's also tracker-first. The counter and pledges are the heart of it. If what you need at 2am is someone to talk you down — a voice, a breathing exercise, a structured daily lesson to work through — that's not the job it was built to do. And some people find a very large community feed motivating; others find it noisy when they're raw. Only you know which one you are.

How Day One is different

Turn Someday Into Day One

The one with a side for the person who loves them

Built by one person who was addicted for 38 years and got free at 50 — then built the app he'd needed the whole time. It covers 13 habit tracks: alcohol, porn, smoking, substances, gambling, social media, gaming, food, shopping, work, anger, supporting someone, and other.

There are SOS tools for the 2am moment — guided breathing, grounding, and recorded "talk me through it" voices. Friendly, an AI recovery companion you can talk to at any hour (that one's part of Pro). Daily 30-day lesson programs with listen-along audio.

And the thing nobody else built: a whole private side for the partner — the person lying awake wondering what to do. Any gender, any relationship. Their side is private; what they write is theirs alone.

Which should you pick?

If what keeps you going is a big community and the daily pledge ritual, I Am Sober is a genuinely good app and you should keep it — plenty of people use it for years and never need anything else.

If you want more than a counter — lessons, a companion to talk to, tools for the worst moments — or there's a person who loves you drowning in this alongside you, that's exactly the gap we built for. Try both free versions this week and keep the one you actually open.
