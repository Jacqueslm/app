Why you can't answer it from where you're standing

You're outside the thing you're trying to measure. He tells you it was two. You found four. He was fine at dinner and gone by ten. You've spent months building a case in your head and then talking yourself out of it by morning.

That's not you being unfair or dramatic. It's what happens when the only information you get is filtered through the person you're asking about.

So put the label down for a minute. It isn't the useful question.

The useful question is: what is this doing to your life? The counting. The bracing. The way you plan your evening around his mood. You don't need a diagnosis to know that's real, and you don't need his permission to do something about your own side of it.

If tonight is worse than that

If you're in crisis, or you've had thoughts of hurting yourself, please talk to a person tonight — not an app. Call or text 988 (US), any hour, free and confidential.

If you're afraid of him — not just hurt, afraid — the National Domestic Violence Hotline is 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788. You don't have to be in danger tonight to call. You're allowed to call just to ask whether what you're living with is normal.

And if you're worried about what happens to his body if he stops drinking suddenly, that's a question for a doctor, not a website. Ask one before he quits cold.
