Full disclosure: this page lives on the site of one of the apps compared — ours. We'll still be straight with you about both, because different apps genuinely fit different people, and the fastest way to lose your trust would be to pretend otherwise.

Loosid answers a question most recovery apps ignore: once the drinking stops, what does Friday night look like? If you're comparing it to other apps, it helps to know it's playing a different game than most of them — including ours.

What Loosid does well

Loosid

The sober social network

Loosid is less a recovery program and more a life. Sober dating, sober events, group chats, travel ideas, people to actually meet — it takes the loneliest part of getting sober and fills it with faces. For someone whose entire social world used to run through bars, that's not a small thing; it can be the difference between a sobriety you endure and one you enjoy.

Free basics · paid tiers — check their site for current pricing

Where it may not fit

Loosid shines once you're steady on your feet. If you're in the early white-knuckle days — the stage where the hard part isn't Friday night plans but getting through Tuesday at 2am — a social network isn't really built for that moment. There's no structured daily program to work through, and no toolkit for the urge that arrives when the house goes quiet.

And like almost everything in this space, it's for the person in recovery. The partner standing beside them isn't part of the picture.

How Day One is different

Turn Someday Into Day One

The one with a side for the person who loves them

Built by one person who was addicted for 38 years and got free at 50 — then built the app he'd needed the whole time. It covers 13 habit tracks: alcohol, porn, smoking, substances, gambling, social media, gaming, food, shopping, work, anger, supporting someone, and other.

There are SOS tools for the 2am moment — guided breathing, grounding, and recorded "talk me through it" voices. Friendly, an AI recovery companion you can talk to at any hour (that one's part of Pro). Daily 30-day lesson programs with listen-along audio.

And the thing nobody else built: a whole private side for the partner — the person lying awake wondering what to do. Any gender, any relationship. Their side is private; what they write is theirs alone.

Which should you pick?

This might be the one comparison on our site where the answer is often both. They barely overlap. If you're months in and the problem is loneliness — you want sober friends, sober dates, somewhere to go — Loosid is the better tool for that, plainly.

If you're at the beginning, still fighting the habit day by day, or you're the one who loves somebody in that fight, start with Day One. Do the daily work here; build the social life there when you're ready. Nobody says you have to choose one app for your whole recovery.
