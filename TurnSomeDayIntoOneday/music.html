The manual How your music gets here

All of it happens on this phone. Nothing is uploaded, and nothing is sent anywhere.

  1. Tap “Tap to choose songs” and pick the files already on the phone. Any of the usual kinds — mp3, m4a, wav, ogg — and as many as you like at once.
  2. Or tap “Scan for new music”. It looks in two folders only — your Music folder and your Download folder — and in the meditation room, and it adds only what is not here yet. The same song in both folders is added once.
  3. They land in “All songs” and are kept on this phone, so the list is waiting the next time you open the page.
  4. Tap any row to play it. The player at the top follows whatever you pick. A song you add today lands at the top of the list — the Order chips flip that round.
  5. To take one off, tap the ✕ on its row. On a playlist the ✕ takes only the row out — the song itself stays on the phone.
  6. To make a playlist, name one under “Playlists”, then tap + on any song. The same song can go in as many times as you like.
  7. A book is not a song. An audiobook is added here like any other file — the −15s and +30s buttons, the speed and the place it remembers on the player card are what it needs. A PDF, a text or a Markdown file has no sound in it, so it is read out loud on the Read tab instead, a paragraph at a time, and it remembers where it stopped.

      How it plays through

      Fade, shuffle and repeat

      The lock screen shows what is playing and carries play, pause and next, so the music carries on with the screen off.

      Fade between songs

      Off means the next song starts the moment this one ends. The choice is kept on the phone.

      Shuffle

      Repeat

      Shuffle plays them in a different order each time. Repeat all starts the whole list again after the last song; Repeat one plays the same song round and round.

      Cut one out

      Ringtone maker

      Choose a song, drag the two handles to the part you want, listen to it, then save the clip. A ringtone sounds best at 15–30 seconds.

      Saves a WAV file of the clip. On an Android phone you can set it as your ringtone from the file. On an iPhone it needs to be an .m4r in the Ringtone folder, so keep it as a sound for now.
