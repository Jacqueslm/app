My partner drinks too much

Whoever they are to you — you're allowed to say it's too much.

Your husband. Your wife. Your boyfriend, your girlfriend, the person you've built a life with. The words change; the nights don't. You're here because somewhere between "everyone drinks" and tonight, it stopped feeling like everyone, and started feeling like a problem with your name on the worry.

"Too much" is not a number

People will tell you what counts as too much — units, nights per week, whether they miss work. None of that is your question. Your question is why you count their drinks and they don't. Why you know the sound of a bottle opening from two rooms away. Why the evening has a mood forecast and you're the one who checks it.

If drinking is costing you peace, that's already "too much" — whatever the number is.

You don't need them to agree with that. You don't need anyone to certify it. What's happening to you is something you're allowed to take seriously all by yourself.

This page is for every "us"

Most of what's written about loving someone who drinks assumes a wife worrying about a husband. Sometimes that's exactly it. And sometimes it's a man worried about his wife, a boyfriend worried about a boyfriend, a girlfriend watching her girlfriend pour the third glass, someone whose partner doesn't fit a box at all.

Same 2 a.m. Same rehearsed conversation. Same hope, same going back on it.

Recovery spaces haven't always felt safe for LGBTQ+ people, and plenty of apps still write as if every couple looks one way. This one doesn't assume. Your relationship is your relationship; the help works the same.

If tonight is worse than that

If you're in crisis, or you've had thoughts of hurting yourself, please talk to a person tonight — not an app. Call or text 988 (US), any hour, free and confidential.

If you're afraid of your partner — not just hurt, afraid — the National Domestic Violence Hotline is 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788. They talk to people of every gender, in every kind of relationship. You don't have to be in danger tonight to call.

And if you're worried about what happens to their body if they stop drinking suddenly, that's a question for a doctor, not a website. Ask one before they quit cold.
