My partner is addicted to porn

You found out. And now you can't un-know it.

Maybe it was the history bar. Maybe the phone turned face-down one time too many, or the shower that runs long, or you just asked straight out and watched the answer arrive too slowly. However you got here — your boyfriend, your husband, your wife, your girlfriend — something shifted when you found out, and pretending it didn't isn't working.

What you're feeling has a name

A lot of people in your spot describe the same strange pair of feelings: it's "just" a screen, so you tell yourself you're overreacting — and at the same time it hurts in the exact place cheating would hurt. The comparing. The wondering what's wrong with you. Checking a phone you never used to check, and hating that you've become someone who checks.

People call that betrayal trauma. You didn't invent it, and you're not dramatic for feeling it.

You don't have to decide tonight whether it "counts" as an addiction, or whether you're allowed to be this hurt. The hurt is already here. The only real question is what you do with it — and doing it alone at 1 a.m. with a search bar hasn't been working.

Whoever you are, whoever they are

Almost everything written about this assumes a wife who found her husband's history. Often enough, that's exactly who's reading. But it's also the boyfriend who found his girlfriend's, the man whose boyfriend hides his phone, the woman whose wife swears it's nothing — every kind of couple, every gender, LGBTQ+ and straight alike.

The discovery feels the same. The 1 a.m. searching feels the same.

This app doesn't assume what your relationship looks like. It just takes seriously what finding out did to you.

The part built for you

Recovery apps for this are built for the person with the habit — blockers, streaks, filters. Fine for them. None of it is for you. This one has a section for the person on the other side of the door:

  • What's yours to carry, and what was never yours
  • The difference between their recovery and your healing — you get your own
  • What checking their phone actually does to you, and what to do instead
  • Language for the conversation that keeps going wrong
  • A private place to put all of it that they never see

Your side works whether they ever change or not.

If tonight is worse than that

If you're in crisis, or you've had thoughts of hurting yourself, please talk to a person tonight — not an app. Call or text 988 (US), any hour, free and confidential.

If what you found involved anything beyond adults, or you're afraid of your partner — afraid, not just hurt — those are bigger than any app. The National Domestic Violence Hotline is 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788, for people of every gender and every kind of relationship.
