The honest summary

\n

Vaping is very probably less harmful than smoking. That is the strongest claim anyone can make for it, and it is a comparison, not a clean bill of health. "Better than the worst thing" is not the same as "fine".

\n

The bigger problem for most people is not the vapour. It is the dose.

Why it hooks harder than cigarettes

\n

A cigarette ends. It burns down, you put it out, and there is a natural stop built into it. A vape has no end. You can hold it all day, and most people do.

\n

That changes the whole shape of the habit. A pack-a-day smoker had twenty moments. A vaper has hundreds — a pull at every red light, every awkward pause, every time a thought gets uncomfortable. Nicotine stops being a habit with a time and becomes the thing you do instead of feeling anything.

\n

It is also invisible and nearly odourless, which means nothing in your life ever pushes back. Nobody makes you stand outside. Nobody says anything. Days go by with no friction at all, and friction is most of what makes people quit.

What is known, and what is not

\n

Known: nicotine is strongly addictive, it raises heart rate and blood pressure, and it affects the developing brain — which is why the teenage numbers worry people who study this for a living.

\n

Known: the vapour is not water. It carries nicotine, flavouring compounds and fine particles into your lungs several hundred times a day.

\n

Not known: what twenty years of that does. These devices have not existed long enough for anyone to tell you honestly, and anybody who says otherwise in either direction is guessing.

\n

You are allowed to decide that unknown is not a bet you want to keep making.

\n

Why quitting feels worse than you expected

\n

Because you are not just removing nicotine. You are removing the thing you have been using to get through every small unpleasant moment for years, and you are removing it from all of those moments at once.

\n

The first three days are chemical — irritable, foggy, restless, sleeping badly. That part ends. What comes after is the real work: several hundred moments a day that used to have an answer and now do not.

What actually helps

\n

Stop trying to quit the vape. Quit the moments, one at a time.

\n
    \n
  • Find the three you reach for hardest — the car, the break, the first minute after work — and give those three something else first. Leave the rest alone for now.
    • \n
  • Put it somewhere with friction. A drawer, a bag, another room. Not your pocket. Most pulls are not cravings, they are proximity.
    • \n
  • Count days somewhere you can see the number. Vaping leaves no evidence behind — no ashtray, no smell, no empty packs — so quitting leaves no evidence either unless you make some.
    • \n
  • Nicotine replacement is not cheating and neither is tapering. The measure is whether you are still holding it in six months.
    • \n
  • Tell one person. Zero friction is the whole reason this one runs for years, and one person who knows is friction.
