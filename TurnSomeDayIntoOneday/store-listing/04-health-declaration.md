# Health apps declaration — answers

Play Console → App content → Health apps declaration.

This form is where the app is either a **wellness tool** or a **medical app**.
The difference is not cosmetic: medical apps carry regulatory-clearance
expectations this app cannot and should not meet. Every answer below is written
to place it, accurately, in the first group.

---

## Does your app have health features?

**Yes.**

Do not answer no. A day counter, mood tracking and a wellbeing journal are
health features, and Play finding an undeclared one later is far worse than
declaring it now.

---

## Which health feature category?

Choose, under **Health and fitness**:

> **Stress management, relaxation, mental acuity**

**Do not choose "Mental and behavioral health"** — that sits under the
**Medical** group, which is the category that pulls regulatory expectations and
the stricter account requirements. This app manages habits and self-reported
wellbeing. It does not assess, diagnose, or manage a condition, and nothing in
it is presented as clinical.

Leave every **Medical** and **Human Subjects Research** box unticked.

---

## Health Connect

**Not used.** The app reads no Health Connect data types and requests no health
permissions. Nothing to justify.

---

## Regulatory clearance

**No — the app has no FDA clearance, CE mark, or equivalent, and does not claim any.**

Because there is no clearance, the store description **must** carry the
disclaimer. It is the first paragraph of `02-full-description.md`:

> This app is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or
> prevent any medical condition. It is a wellness and habit-support tool. If you
> are struggling with your health, please consult a qualified healthcare
> professional.

Non-regulated health apps that omit or bury this get updates rejected.

## Free-text description of health features — draft

```
This app provides general wellness and habit-support features. Users can track how many days they have been working on a habit, read a short daily lesson, keep a private journal with an optional mood note, and use guided breathing and grounding exercises during difficult moments.

The app does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition, and is not a medical device. It provides no clinical assessment, no diagnosis, no treatment plan, and no medical advice. It does not connect users to licensed clinicians and does not act as an emergency or crisis service. Users are directed to qualified healthcare professionals for health concerns, and to emergency services and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline in a crisis.

The app accesses no Health Connect data types and requests no health permissions. Self-reported wellbeing data stays in the user's own account and is not sold or shared.
```

## Policies this category triggers — what to actually watch

1. **Health Content and Services policy.** Applies from the moment you tick
   "Yes" above. The disclaimer requirement and the ban on unsupported health
   claims both come from here.

2. **No outcome claims, anywhere.** Not in the description, not in screenshots,
   not in the app. "Helps you quit", "proven to work", "reduces cravings",
   "improves mental health" are all claims that require evidence you do not
   have. The description was written with none, deliberately.

3. **Sensitive data handling.** Ticking Health info in the data safety form
   means Play holds you to it. The privacy policy must match the form exactly —
   they are read together.

4. **Account type — verify this yourself before you rely on it.** You have ruled
   that Organization accounts are required only for Medical and Human Subjects
   Research apps, and this is neither. That reading matches how the categories
   are grouped on the declaration form. I could not confirm it against Google's
   own page — `support.google.com` refuses automated fetches from this
   environment — and several secondary write-ups state the requirement more
   broadly than your reading does. **Check the wording in the console when you
   submit the declaration.** If the form says otherwise, stop and tell me, because
   it changes the whole plan.

5. **Crisis content.** Play scrutinises apps in this space for what they do when
   a user is in danger. The description names emergency services and 988, and the
   app's SOS path does too. Keep it that way.
