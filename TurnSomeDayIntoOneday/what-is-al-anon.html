What is Al-Anon?

It's the meeting for everyone else in the house.

AA is for the person drinking. Al-Anon is for the people around them — the wife, the husband, the partner, the mother, the grown-up child who still picks up the phone at midnight. It has been running since 1951, and the whole idea is that the family gets hurt by this too, and gets to have somewhere of their own to take it.

What actually happens at a meeting

Far less than people brace for. You turn up, usually to a church hall, a community room, or a video call. Somebody reads a few opening lines. People take turns talking about their own week — what happened, what they did with it — and nobody interrupts, gives advice, or tells anyone to leave their marriage.

You do not have to speak. You can say "I'll just listen today" and that is a completely normal thing to say.

You don't have to give your surname. What's said in the room is meant to stay in the room — that's the anonymous part, and it's taken seriously. Most people describe the first meeting the same way: quieter than expected, and a strange relief at hearing somebody describe their own house out loud.

What it costs, and what they'll want from you

  • It's free. No fees, no dues. A basket goes around for voluntary contributions toward the room's rent — you can pass it straight on
  • No signing up, no membership form, no commitment to come back
  • Nobody will call you. You're not on a list
  • You don't have to be married to them, or living with them, or still with them at all
  • Your person doesn't have to be sober, in treatment, or even willing to admit anything. Al-Anon is for you regardless of what they do

Meetings run in person, by phone and online, at most hours of the day and night — the online and phone ones matter if you can't easily leave the house, or you'd rather not be seen walking in somewhere local.

Is it religious?

It's a twelve-step program, so it talks about a "higher power," and some meetings say a prayer at the end. But it isn't tied to any church, and how you read that phrase is left entirely to you — plenty of members are not religious at all and simply take it to mean "something other than my own willpower."

If one meeting doesn't suit you, another will. They vary enormously.

Worth knowing: meetings differ more than the name suggests — different rooms, different hours, different feel. People who say "I tried it and it wasn't for me" often tried exactly one. If you can, try three before you decide.

The nights between meetings

Here's the honest gap, and it's not a criticism of Al-Anon — it's just how a weekly meeting works. The meeting is Tuesday. The bad night is Saturday, at 1 a.m., when they're not home and you've read the same text eleven times.

If your person is the one drinking

Al-Anon is for you, not for them — they'd be looking at AA, or a doctor. Two things worth knowing if they're thinking about stopping: heavy daily drinking is one of the few things it can be genuinely dangerous to quit cold turkey, and that's a conversation with a doctor, not an app. And nobody gets talked into it from a standing start.

If tonight is worse than that

If you're in crisis, or you've had thoughts of hurting yourself, please talk to a person tonight — not an app. Call or text 988 (US), any hour, free and confidential.

If you're afraid of your partner — afraid, not just worn down — the National Domestic Violence Hotline is 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788, for people of every gender and every kind of relationship.
