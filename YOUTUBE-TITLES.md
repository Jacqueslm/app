# YOUTUBE TITLES — only where the hook and the search term differ

**Why this file exists.** The first line of your Buffer caption becomes the
YouTube title. That line has two different jobs depending on where it lands:

- **TikTok / Facebook** — it has to stop a thumb. A hook wins.
- **YouTube** — it has to match what somebody typed into a search box. A hook
  loses, because nobody searches "the package doesn't fix it".

So on YouTube, and only on YouTube, use the title below instead. In Buffer,
press **Customize for each network**, pick the YouTube channel, and replace
the first line. TikTok and Facebook keep the hook exactly as written in
SCRIPTS.md.

Every phrase in bold is a real term from KEYWORDS.md with its monthly US
volume and difficulty. Nothing here is invented — where no honest term
exists, the script is listed at the bottom and keeps its hook everywhere.

## No honest term exists — keep the hook everywhere

3, 5, 7, 20, 21, 23, 24, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31, 33, 38, 39.

These are hooks with no matching search demand in KEYWORDS.md. Inventing a
keyword-shaped title for them would be guessing, and a title that promises a
search answer the video doesn't give is worse than no traffic. Post them as
written — they earn their reach on the feed, not in search.

Two worth noting: **31** (gambling) and **33** (smoking) cover real tracks in
the app, but KEYWORDS.md has no Build term for either. That is a gap in the
research, not a reason to make one up.

## The rule, in one line

If the hook already contains a phrase somebody would type, leave it. If it
doesn't, put the search phrase first and the hook second — the em dash does
the work.
