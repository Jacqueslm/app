# Shorts / Reels / Cards — content pack

Engagement-first content built from the three partner angles (her side, his
side, together) plus the core user. No new app features required — every
message here works as a video or a card today.

**How to shoot each one in Studio (all free):**
1. Post tab → paste the script into the Teleprompter → Save script.
2. Open teleprompter → tap 🎥 Record my webcam on the prompter bar
   (5–10s countdown, blur background if the room's a mess).
3. Library → transcribe (local Whisper) for captions → reframe to 9:16
   → audio remaster → done.
4. Post tab → schedule it. Post native, not via API — better reach anyway.

**Voice rules (matches the app + the claims audit):**
- Spoken words, not written ones. Contractions. No "research shows,"
  no dopamine talk, no "clinically proven." Your story, not statistics.
- One idea per video. 15–35 seconds. The hook is the first line —
  say it looking at the lens, no intro, no "hey guys."
- Soft CTA only: the app name on screen at the end, or nothing at all.
  Cards carry the name in the corner.

## LANE A — the man in it (core user)

### A1 — "Day one, again"
HOOK: I've had more day ones than I can count.

That used to be the most shameful sentence I owned.

Now it's the reason I built a system
instead of relying on willpower.

Willpower gets you to Tuesday.
A system gets you to day ninety.

If today is day one again —
good. You showed back up.

### A2 — "The hiding is the habit"
HOOK: The habit isn't the worst part. The hiding is.

Deleting history.
Timing it around her schedule.
Rehearsing your face before you walk out of the room.

That performance costs more than the thing itself.

The day I stopped hiding from myself
was the first day anything actually moved.

### A3 — "Nobody claps on day 4"
HOOK: Nobody claps for you on day four.

Day thirty gets a milestone.
Day ninety gets a story.

Day four gets nothing —
except it's the day most people quit.

So this is me clapping for your day four.
Keep going. It counts the most.

## LANE B — for her (partner of the man in it)

### B1 — "It's not yours to carry"
HOOK: You didn't cause it. You can't cure it. You're allowed to stop trying.

Every app in this space is built for him.

Almost nothing is built for the person
lying awake next to him.

What's actually yours to carry,
what isn't,
and how to tell if anything is really changing —

that deserves its own answer.
Not a footnote in his.

### B2 — "Checking his phone"
HOOK: Checking his phone never once made you feel better.

Maybe you found nothing — didn't help.
Maybe you found something — definitely didn't help.

Because what you're looking for
isn't on the phone.

It's whether he's doing the work
when nobody's checking.

You can't verify that from history tabs.
And you were never supposed to have to.

## LANE C — for him (man whose partner is struggling)

### C1 — "You can't fix her like a project"
HOOK: You can't manage someone out of an addiction. I tried.

Men get handed one tool: fix it.

So we track it, control it,
turn the person we love into a project.

And she reads every bit of that as
"you're the problem."

Walking beside someone
and carrying them
are not the same thing.

One of them works.

### C2 — "Who did you tell?"
HOOK: Quick question — who have you told?

If your wife drank too much,
if your girlfriend can't put the vape down —

who knows?

For most men the honest answer is: nobody.

You're handling the hardest thing in your life
in total silence.

That silence isn't strength.
It's just weight.

## LANE D — together (couples / accountability)

### D1 — "Same house, same fight"
HOOK: She wants him to quit. He wants her to quit. Nobody wants to go first.

Here's what happens when one person
polices the other:
resentment, in both directions.

Here's what happens when two people
each pick their own thing
and put their own skin in the game:

nobody's the warden.
Everybody's in it.

Same house. Same fight. Different battles.

### D2 — "Don't be the warden"
HOOK: The fastest way to lose them is to become their probation officer.

Report cards create liars.
Not because they're bad people —
because shame hides.

Support looks like this:
I see you showing up.
I'm not counting your failures.
I've got my own thing I'm working on too.

That's the whole trick.

## Cadence (keep it boring and repeatable)

- 3 videos a week, rotating lanes (A, then B or C, then D or another A).
- 2 cards a week on the off days.
- Same hook style every time — first line is the thesis, said to the lens.
- Batch-record: one sitting, 3 scripts, teleprompter + countdown + blur.
  Studio saves the scripts, so re-record a flopped one any time.
- Comments on B and C lanes are the leads — those people are the pages
  (/for-her today, /for-him when it exists). Reply like a person, never
  paste a link unless asked.
