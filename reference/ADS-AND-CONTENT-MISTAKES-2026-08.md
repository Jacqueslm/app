# Advertising & social-content mistakes — research, 6 Aug 2026

Requested by Jacques. Sources: live Semrush pulls (US) + web research, August
2026. Context rule that still stands (DO-THIS-NEXT / marketer agent): **no paid
ads until organic shows a converting angle in /admin/stats.** This file is
preparation, not a green light.

---

## 1. What Semrush shows about paid ads in OUR exact niche

**Nobody is currently bidding on "quit drinking app" or "sobriety app"**
(phrase_adwords returned zero live advertisers). Historical bidders on "quit
drinking app": joinreframeapp.com and a hypnosis app. The recovery-app paid
market is thin — which cuts both ways: cheap clicks if we ever go paid, but
also a warning that the players who tried mostly stopped.

**Reframe's entire Google Ads playbook** (resource_adwords on
joinreframeapp.com, sorted by traffic):
- ~70% of their paid traffic is them **bidding on their own brand term**
  ("reframe app", 206+39 clicks/mo) — defense, not growth.
- Everything else is long-tail at 0–5 clicks/mo: "app stop drinking",
  "alcohol reduction app", "weaning off of alcohol", "quitting drinking
  without aa", "how i stopped drinking wine every night".
- Their copy leans entirely on **claims we can't and won't make**: "91% of
  users reported lower consumption", "science-backed", "created by leading
  experts", "#1 app". That's their moat in ads — and our claims-audit rules
  make it a lane we never fight in. Our ad lane, if it ever opens, is the
  same as our organic lane: story and the partner angle, where they have
  nothing.
- CPCs in the niche run **$1–$4.40** (quit drinking app $3.29, sunnyside
  brand $6.43). A $5/day test = 1–3 clicks/day. See mistake #1 below for
  why that's a trap.

## 2. The mistakes list — paid ads (small advertisers)

1. **Spending too little to learn anything, then concluding "ads don't
   work."** Platforms have a learning phase; Meta wants ~50 conversions/month
   per ad set to optimize. $10/day spread across several ad sets never exits
   learning. The honest minimum test is one ad set, one audience, one goal,
   run long enough to get real numbers — or don't start.
2. **Running ads before organic converts.** If the funnel (video → bio link →
   /app → signup) isn't converting for free traffic, paid traffic just pays
   for the same leak. This is why the "no ads until /admin/stats shows a
   converting angle" rule exists — it's not caution, it's sequencing.
3. **Targeting everyone.** "US, 18–65" is not an audience. Ours would be
   painfully specific (e.g. women 30–55, interests overlapping Al-Anon/
   sobriety content) — except see #6, because in our category interest
   targeting on health conditions is exactly what gets ads rejected.
4. **Mixing objectives in one campaign** (awareness + clicks + installs)
   — confuses the optimizer, burns budget.
5. **Stock imagery.** Real faces and real footage outperform stock heavily.
   We already hold this card: Jacques' face, his stills, his story.
6. **THE CATEGORY TRAP (biggest one for us):** "addiction treatment" is a
   restricted category. Google requires **LegitScript certification** (30–60
   days, annual renewal, aimed at treatment providers) for
   addiction-treatment ads — no exceptions; Meta requires LegitScript AND
   written permission for US addiction-treatment ads. One policy misstep can
   suspend an ad account. **How the apps that DO advertise get around it:**
   they position as *habit change / wellness*, never treatment — Reframe's
   copy is "drink less, live more", "mindful drinking", "break your drinking
   habit". Never "addiction", "recovery from alcoholism", "treatment".
7. **Personal-attributes copy.** Meta prohibits ads implying knowledge of the
   viewer's condition. "Are you struggling with addiction?" / "Do you drink
   too much?" = rejected (or worse, flagged account). Allowed shape:
   "Support exists" / "The app for cutting back". Our whole HER lane
   ("worried about someone you love") is *safer* than the HIM lane here —
   it addresses the supporter, not the sufferer's condition.
8. **No fast follow-up on leads.** A lead that waits a day is gone. Our
   funnel is app-signup (instant), so this mostly means: the welcome email
   must fire immediately (it does, via Resend).

## 3. The mistakes list — organic short-form content

1. **Weak first three seconds.** Intro retention (making it past ~3s) is the
   metric the algorithm watches hardest; strong creators clear 70%. The hook
   IS the video. Our scripts already lead with the hook line — keep that
   discipline, never open with "hey everyone" or a logo.
2. **Hooks that don't pay off.** Curiosity-gap openers that under-deliver
   teach the algorithm the account wastes time → less distribution. Say the
   honest thing the hook promised.
3. **Ignoring mute viewers.** A large share watch with sound OFF first.
   Captions on everything (Studio does this), and the first visual has to
   work silent — a face, a bold on-screen line.
4. **Volume over quality.** 2026 consensus: 3–5 good videos/week beats daily
   low-effort posting. Jacques' schedule (3 slots/day from a banked pool of
   *finished* videos, 4 new/week max) is already on the right side of this —
   do not let anyone talk him into "post 5× daily".
5. **Chasing trends/sounds outside the lane.** Trend-hopping reads as noise
   and pulls the account's identity apart. His lane is fixed: his face, his
   story, the partner angle.
6. **List-structure retention.** "3 things / 5 mistakes" formats hold viewers
   to the end (they wait to check the next item). Cheap, honest retention
   device — works fine with the VICTORY tone. Worth using in a few upcoming
   scripts.
7. **Posting into the feed and walking away.** Comment replies in the first
   hour matter for distribution. Covered: his wife runs comments from the
   dashboards.

## 4. What this means for us, concretely

- **Now (still $0 paid):** everything in section 3 is already mostly our
  practice; the two upgrades worth adopting are (a) mute-proof first frames —
  bold on-screen hook text in the first second, and (b) a few list-format
  scripts ("3 promises that aren't changes", "5 things that aren't yours to
  carry").
- **When /admin/stats ever shows a converting angle:** the ad-safe wedge is
  the HER/partner lane with habit-change language (never treatment, never
  "are you...?" copy), tiny scope (one ad set, one audience, one objective),
  real footage, and a landing page that already converts organically
  (/partner-drinks or /when-he-drinks). CPCs in the niche say $100 buys
  ~25–70 clicks — enough for a signal only if the page converts.
- **Never:** LegitScript-territory copy (treatment claims), stat claims
  ("91%", "science-backed") — Reframe can have that lane; ours is the story.
