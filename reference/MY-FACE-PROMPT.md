# Keeping your own face across every shot

Two routes. **Route A** is the one you asked for — a prompt for ChatGPT. **Route
B** is stronger and you already own it. Use A today, B once it's worth ten
minutes.

---

# ROUTE A — the ChatGPT prompt

## Step 1 — paste this ONCE, with 3–5 photos of yourself attached

Attach photos taken in **different light and from different angles** — one
straight on, one three-quarter, one in poor light. Same beard length in all of
them. Then paste:

> These photos are of me. I own this likeness and I'm using it in marketing for
> my own app, so please use them as the reference for every image I ask for in
> this conversation.
>
> Build a reference description of me from these photos and repeat it back, so
> we're both working from the same one. Cover: face shape, skin tone, beard
> shape and length, hairline, eye shape, build, and age.
>
> House look for every image, unless I say otherwise:
> vertical 9:16, photographic, documentary realism, warm practical light from
> lamps or windows only — no studio lighting, no rim light, no lens flare.
> Muted color, soft contrast, shallow depth of field, shot on 35mm. An
> ordinary American home or street. Nothing futuristic, no neon, no holograms,
> no floating screens, no text in the image.
>
> Never make me look triumphant, heroic or fitness-model. Tired, ordinary and
> real is always right.
>
> Confirm you've got it, then wait for my first scene.

## Step 2 — every scene after that is one line

> Scene: [what I'm doing, where, what time of day]. Same person, same house
> look.

That's it. Keep it in **one conversation** — start a new chat and the face is
gone.

## The scenes worth having

Ask for these one at a time, not in a batch — batches drift.

| # | Scene |
|---|---|
| 1 | Me leaning on a bathroom sink, looking at myself in the mirror, late at night, one vanity light on |
| 2 | Me sitting on the edge of a bed in the dark, elbows on knees, phone face-down beside me |
| 3 | Me at a kitchen table before sunrise, both hands around a mug, not drinking it |
| 4 | Me standing at a window in gray early light, curtains half open |
| 5 | Me in a parked car outside a house, hands on the wheel, engine off, streetlight through the windscreen |
| 6 | Me on a front porch step at dusk, phone in hand, not looking at it |
| 7 | Close on my hands writing in a cheap notebook at a kitchen table |
| 8 | Me laughing at something off-camera in a lit kitchen, ordinary evening |

**Ask for three versions of each** — near, mid, wide, same room. Three angles of
one scene makes a 13-second short. One frame held twice makes a poster.

Shot 7 has no face in it, so it will come back right every time. Shot 8 is the
only one that isn't heavy — you need at least one, or the whole account reads
as despair.

## What will go wrong, and what to do

- **It refuses.** Some versions won't render a real person from photos. Say:
  *"This is my own face and my own app — I'm the rights holder."* If it still
  refuses, drop the photos and use the written description from Step 1 instead.
  You lose exactness and keep consistency, which is most of the value.
- **The face drifts after a few images.** Paste the description back in and
  say *"back to this face."*
- **It adds text to the image.** It will get the words wrong. Always say **no
  text in the image** — captions go on in Studio, where you control them.
- **It makes you look like a model.** Say *"more tired, less handsome, ordinary
  lighting."* Every generator pulls toward glamour and it is exactly wrong here.

# ROUTE B — Studio's face lock, which you already have

Studio has **🧬 face-lock** built in (Characters card). You upload reference
photos of yourself, press Train, and it learns your face into a lock that every
generated shot uses. It costs a few dollars from your fal balance and takes
about ten minutes, once.

**Why it beats Route A:** it doesn't drift, it doesn't refuse, it doesn't need
the same chat window, and it works months from now — the same face in January
as today. Route A forgets you the moment the conversation ends.

**When to do it:** after the six talking heads are shot and posted. Not before —
nothing on this list matters more than getting your real face on camera this
week.

---

## The rule that decides which face to use

**Anything in "I" voice — your face. Anything in "you" voice — either.**

"Nobody talked me out of it" is your life and needs to be you. "The hardest
person to be honest with is yourself" is about the viewer, so a generated
stand-in is fine and honest.

And when you post a generated one, tick **Made with AI** — Studio appends it to
the caption automatically on the AI presets. Labelling costs nothing. Getting
caught not labelling costs the account.
