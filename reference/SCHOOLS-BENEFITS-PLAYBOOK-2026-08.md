# Schools / benefits-department outreach — the playbook (researched 6 Aug 2026)

The 5 Aug session's verdict stands and this research confirms it: **do not
enter procurement.** The winning path is the free staff-wellness pilot, person
to person, below every dollar threshold. This file is the how.

---

## The numbers that make the pitch work (all 2025-26, cite freely — they're
about the problem, not about our app, so the no-claims rule doesn't apply)

- RAND 2025: **53% of K-12 teachers report burnout**; 57% report frequent
  job stress vs 40% of other working adults.
- 88% of teachers work 41-80+ hours against 21-40-hour contracts.
- Replacing ONE teacher costs a district **$11,860-$24,930**. That's the
  budget line the benefits person is judged on.
- Over half of districts added a mental-health resource since 2020 — the
  norm is set, budgets exist, and coordinators are actively collecting
  options. An addiction/recovery angle is one almost none of them have.

## Who the person actually is

Not the superintendent, not the board. The titles that own staff wellness:
- **Benefits Coordinator / Benefits Specialist** (HR dept) — runs the EAP
  contract and the benefits fair.
- **Employee Wellness Coordinator** (bigger districts have a dedicated one;
  e.g. Fairfax County PS runs a whole Employee Wellness Program office).
- **Director of Human Resources** (small districts — HR is 1-2 people and
  this person is everything).
Decision power for a FREE resource sits right there. Money decisions above
~$5,000 escalate; free never escalates. That is the whole trick.

## How to find them in 10 minutes (no tool needed)

1. Google: `"[district name]" employee wellness` or `"[district name]" HR
   benefits staff`. District staff directories are public.
2. On the district site: Departments → Human Resources → Benefits. The page
   almost always lists names, titles, and direct emails (public sector —
   emails are usually firstname.lastname@district.k12.STATE.us).
3. No name listed? The HR main line answers "who coordinates employee
   wellness?" — receptionists at districts route this happily.
4. Start local: OUR county's districts first. "I'm local" beats any funnel.

## What they care about (in their order, not ours)

1. **Is it free / what's the catch** — say the price of the pilot ($0) and
   the price after (the app's normal consumer pricing, individual choice,
   nothing billed to the district) in the FIRST email. Vagueness = deleted.
2. **Liability & optics** — it must be *opt-in, private, off-district
   systems*. Never roster uploads, never usage reports on individuals.
   Our privacy stance ("nobody sees your data, not even a spouse") is
   exactly what a benefits person wants to forward.
3. **Zero admin work** — they will not run anything. The deliverable is a
   ready-to-paste blurb for the staff newsletter / benefits-fair flyer.
4. **Not "addiction treatment"** — frame as *staff wellbeing resource*
   (stress, habits, supporting a loved one). The supporter angle is the
   door-opener here too: every staff room has people worried about someone.

## The pitch shape that fits all of the above

Free wellness resource for your staff + zero admin (we hand you the blurb) +
private and opt-in + built by one person in recovery, not a vendor. Ask for
nothing but permission to be included in the next staff wellness email.
That's not a sale; it's a favor to their newsletter.

## What kills it

- Pitching the superintendent (gets forwarded down with no champion).
- Asking for a meeting in email one (ask to be *included*, not met).
- Any hint of per-seat pricing to the district (triggers vendor process:
  W-9s, insurance certificates, board approval — months, and we lack the
  paperwork; the 5 Aug session already ruled this out).
- Data promises we can't keep, or treatment language (we're a wellbeing
  app, not a provider).

## Sequencing & expectations

- This is the LATER file of outreach: rehab/media (OUTREACH.md) stays the
  Tuesday priority; schools are the extra-15-minutes pile alongside
  INFLUENCERS.md, 1 email/week.
- Realistic conversion: newsletters go out monthly; a yes can take 4-8
  weeks and land in the next issue. One district saying yes = a repeatable
  template + a name to drop with every neighboring district ("we're in
  [district]'s staff wellness resources").
- The endgame if it works is NOT a district contract — it's dozens of
  newsletter placements, each reaching 500-5,000 staff, at $0 CAC.
