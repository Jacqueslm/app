# Treatment centers & sober living — the B2B play

Built 25 Aug 2026. The reasoning: consumer subscriptions at $9.99 need hundreds
of buyers to matter; one facility at $199/month equals twenty of them, has real
budget, and hands the app to people at the single most motivated moment of their
lives. One-pager: `reference/pitch/onepager.pdf` (attach it, don't paste it).

## The offer

**Free for their clients, paid by the program.** Clients get the whole free tier
forever regardless. The program pays for continuity — their name attached to the
90 days after discharge — and for the family piece.

| Program | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Sober living / recovery residence (≤20 beds) | $99 |
| IOP / outpatient | $199 |
| Residential (20–100 beds) | $349 |
| Multi-site | per location, discounted |

**Opener for the first five: 90 days free**, no card, no contract, cancel by
replying. Asked in return: one honest conversation at the end, and permission to
use their name if it helped. He has no case studies yet — this buys the first
ones, and a free pilot is far easier for a director to say yes to than a budget
request that needs approval.

## Why this lands (all verifiable, all in COMPETITORS.md)

1. **The discharge cliff is their problem, not ours.** Outcomes are measured
   after people leave, and that is exactly where the program loses contact.
2. **Zero of 160 recovery apps serve the family.** Measured on the Play Store,
   Aug 2026. Every family program in America sends people home with a pamphlet.
   This is the only thing on the table that speaks to the wife in the room.
3. **28% of all 1–2★ reviews in the category are about money.** Directors have
   been burned by vendors; "free for your clients, forever" removes the objection
   before it is raised.
4. **Nothing to administer.** No seats, no logins to provision, no IT review, no
   client data flowing to anyone — which also means no BAA/HIPAA fight, because
   we never receive protected health information.

## Who to email (in order of how fast they can say yes)

1. **Sober living / recovery residences** — often owner-operated, decide in one
   email, no committee. Best first target by far.
2. **Family program coordinator / family therapist** at a residential center —
   they feel the gap most, and they are not the person guarding the budget.
3. **Alumni / aftercare coordinator** — their entire job is post-discharge
   contact. This makes their job easier.
4. **Clinical director / program director** — decision maker, slower.
5. Avoid opening with marketing/admissions: they will read it as a vendor pitch.

## Where the list comes from (Jacques, ~20 minutes, no scraping needed)

- **findtreatment.gov** (SAMHSA) — filter by state and service type; every
  licensed facility in the country, with phone and website.
- **Your state's licensed recovery-residence registry** — Missouri: MCRSP /
  MASH-style listings; most states publish a PDF or searchable list.
- **NARR affiliate directories** — the sober-living trade body, state by state.
- **Psychology Today's treatment directory** — has named staff on many listings.
- **Google Maps: "sober living <city>"** — small operators who never show up in
  the official registries but have 6–20 beds and full autonomy.

Qualify in ten seconds: does the site name a real person (director, owner, family
coordinator)? If yes, that's the recipient. If the only contact is info@, send it
there anyway but expect a lower hit rate.

## Objections, and the honest answer to each

- **"Is this HIPAA compliant?"** — No PHI ever reaches us. The app works with no
  account and no name; journals stay on the device. There is nothing to sign
  because there is nothing to share.
- **"We already recommend an app."** — Good. Which one, and does it have anything
  for the family? That's the piece I'd add rather than replace.
- **"What's the catch on free?"** — The recovery tools and the first half of the
  program are free forever, funded by an optional $9.99 subscription for the
  second half and the AI companion. Your clients are never pushed into it, and
  there are no ads.
- **"We don't have budget."** — Then take the 90 days free and decide later. If
  it isn't worth $99 after that, it isn't worth your time either.
- **"Who are you?"** — 38 years in it, free at 50, built this myself, use it
  daily. Happy to get on a call and be asked anything.
