# Marketing Playbook — Turn Someday Into Day One

Everything in one place. No more building until you're in the Play Store.
Read top to bottom once, then work from the STEPS and the NEXT BATCH.

---

## 1. THE PLAY (the whole strategy on one screen)

**Positioning (say this everywhere):**
> The recovery app that includes the person who loves them.

Not "multi-addiction." Not "2am." That one line. Four competitors already
say "track any habit" — nobody says the partner has a place here.

**The front door: HER, flavored to drinking.**
- You market ONE narrow door at a time. They all open into the same app,
  same email list, same subscription.
- First door = **the wife of the man who drinks.** Biggest, least
  stigmatized, ad-safe, and — the key — nobody has ever built a product
  for her (Al-Anon has served her since 1951 with no app).
- She's the buyer (searches in crisis, has money), AND she's your way into
  the crowded alcohol market you can't outspend — she brings him the app.

**Why not the other doors first:**
- Porn = platform-banned, free-first crowd, Quittr owns search. Stays in
  the app, off the sign.
- "Quit drinking" direct = you'd be the 5th new app fighting Reframe
  ($100M) for the most expensive keywords. Don't.
- Couples/both-have-habits = needs 4 conditions to line up. Keep it as a
  content angle, not the door.

**Your validated proof:** your own YouTube — "you are valid too" (supporter
angle) got 428 views; generic "you come first" got 14. Specific wins.

## 3. THE STEPS (do these, in order)

### This week — setup (one sitting each)
1. **Bios:** paste `/go/youtube` into your YouTube channel link, `/go/tiktok`
   into TikTok bio, `/go/facebook` into Facebook. (During weeks you push the
   "her" angle, this is the link. It all tracks automatically.)
2. **Retitle your existing YouTube shorts** in YouTube Studio → Content →
   Shorts. Use search-language titles (the situation, not the encouragement):
   - "you are valid too"   → "When you're supporting someone with an addiction and nobody asks about you"
   - "with someone struggling" → "Loving someone who's struggling is its own kind of tired"
   - "when you are trapped" → "When you feel trapped by someone else's addiction"
   - Rule: every title names HER SITUATION, in words she'd type at 1am.
3. **Fix the YouTube bio** to lead with the empty room, not the banned word:
   > Addicted for 38 years. At 50 it was do or die. I chose Day One — and I
   > built the app I needed, with a side for the person who loves them.
4. **Delete the `test123` test signup:** log into that account → Profile →
   Delete account (works now).

### This week — first content push
5. **Batch-record 3 videos** from the NEXT BATCH below (one teleprompter
   sitting, countdown + blur background). Caption them, reframe 9:16.
6. **Post native** to TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Facebook — not via API. Native
   uploads get more reach and the platforms don't punish them.
7. **Tick "AI-generated" disclosure** on every upload.

### Ongoing — the weekly rhythm (keep it boring)
8. **3 videos + 2 cards per week.** Lead with the HER/drinking lane (it's
   validated); mix in a him/supporter one. Batch-record so it's one sitting.
9. **Work the comments — this is where the leads are.** On the "her" videos,
   commenters asking "what app is this?" get the name (and the link only if
   they ask). Reply like a person, never paste a link cold.
10. **Check `/admin/stats` once a week.** It shows emails by platform. Whichever
    source grows is where you post more. Stop guessing; follow the number.

### Distribution beyond posting (higher-leverage, do after the rhythm is set)
11. **Rehab roundup outreach:** email 25 rehabs/treatment centers that publish
    "best recovery apps" lists. Pitch: "Your list is from 2021 — here's a free
    2026 update, and none of the apps on it support the family." You're giving
    them SEO; they add you because the family angle is the gap.
12. **Free listings:** submit to AlternativeTo against I Am Sober, Nomo, Sober
    Grid, Loosid. Free, indexes "X alternative" searches.
13. **One YouTube long-form** in her exact search language: "My husband drinks
    and I don't know what to do." Ranks for years while shorts churn. (Script
    it in the teleprompter.)

### Only after paid interest shows up
14. **Cheap ads on partner-of-drinker keywords** — never on "quit drinking"
    head terms. Only once `/admin/stats` shows the angle converts organically.

## 4. NEXT BATCH — new scripts to shoot (validated lane first)

### HER — the wife of the man who drinks (lead with these)

**H1 — "I only had two"**
HOOK: "I only had two." You've heard that so many times you could laugh.

You counted. You always count.
Two doesn't leave the recycling like that.

You're not paranoid.
You're just the only sober person keeping track.

That exhaustion has a name,
and there's a place that's actually for you.

**H2 — "The Sunday promise"**
HOOK: He means it every Sunday. It's gone by Friday.

And every week you let yourself believe it a little,
because not believing it costs even more.

Here's what nobody tells you:
you can stop grading your week on his word.

There's a way to tell a promise from a change —
from the outside. That part is yours to learn.

**H3 — "Nobody asks about you"**
HOOK: Everybody asks how he's doing. Nobody asks how you are.

He gets the check-ins. He gets the grace.
You get "you're so strong."

Strong is just the word people use
when they need you to keep carrying it.

You're allowed to put some of it down.
You didn't cause this, and it was never yours to fix.

**H4 — "You went looking"**
HOOK: You checked his phone again. It didn't make you feel better.

It never does.
Because what you're actually looking for
isn't in there.

It's whether he's doing the work
when nobody's watching.

You can't find that in a search history.
And you were never supposed to have to look.

### HIM / SUPPORTER — won on your channel, keep feeding it

**S1 — "You can't manage it"**
HOOK: You can't manage someone out of an addiction. I know. I tried.

Men get handed one tool: fix it.
So we track it, control it, turn her into a project.

And she reads every bit of that as
"you're the problem."

Standing beside someone and carrying them
are not the same thing. Only one works.

**S2 — "Who did you tell?"**
HOOK: Quick question — who have you actually told?

If she drinks too much,
if he can't put it down —
who knows?

For most people the honest answer is nobody.
You're handling the hardest thing in your life in silence.

That silence isn't strength. It's just weight.

### CORE USER — the man in it

That used to be the most shameful sentence I owned.
Now it's the reason I stopped trusting willpower

Willpower gets you to Tuesday.
A system gets you to day ninety.

If today's day one again — good. You showed back up.

## 5. THE MEASUREMENT LOOP (how you know it's working)

Post → short link (`/go/...`) → landing page → email captured →
`/admin/stats` counts it by platform.

Every week, open `/admin/stats` and read three numbers:
- **Leads by source** — which platform is actually delivering emails.
- **Signups** — how many became accounts.
- **Trials** — how many started the 7-day trial.

Double down on whatever line grows. Kill whatever's flat. That's the whole job.

## 6. GUARDRAILS (while in Play Store testing)

- **Web/content changes = safe.** Landing pages, videos, posts, lesson copy —
  none of it touches the Android package or resets your tester clock.
- **Do NOT touch billing** (`store-billing.js` / Stripe) until you're through
  the 12-testers/14-day window — changing purchase behavior mid-review invites
  scrutiny.
- **Keep the live site stable** — testers open the app daily; a broken deploy
  during the window hurts more than a quiet week.
- **Get a few real testers** mixed in with any tester-community ones, and note
  one real piece of feedback you can quote on the production-access form.
- **Data safety form:** if you ever add a feature that shares data between users
  (the partner-link idea), update the form BEFORE it goes live.

No claims copy anywhere should say "research shows," "clinically proven," or
name a brain chemical — your own claims audit already flagged that. Story, not
statistics.
