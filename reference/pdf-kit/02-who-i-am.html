Thirty-eight years. Free at fifty.

I was addicted for thirty-eight years — porn, food, anger. At fifty it was do or die. I got free, and then I built the thing I had needed the whole time and could never find.

It is not a clinic and does not pretend to be. It is the thing I wanted at 2am, built properly.

Why this app exists

The hardest part of recovery was never knowing what I was supposed to do. I knew. The hard part was eleven o'clock at night, on my own with it, with nothing between me and the next decision.

Everything I could find was built for the easy part — the tracking, the charts, the streak you look at on a good day. Nothing was built for the bad ten minutes. So the app is designed for that moment and not for a spreadsheet: an SOS screen you can use hands-free, lessons short enough to finish when your head is not working, and a counter that keeps your lifetime total even after a slip, because one hard day should not erase a hard year.

The half nobody builds for

Families have been living around addiction since long before anyone made software for them — Al-Anon has existed since 1951. Seventy years on, I counted roughly 160 recovery apps on the Play Store and could not find one built for the person on the other side of it.

That person is not a bystander. They lose the evenings, the sleep, and the version of themselves that used to be easy to be around. Nobody asks how they are doing. So they get their own track in this app, in their own voice, about what is actually theirs to carry — boundaries, the anger they learned to predict, and getting their own life back.

It does not ask them to fix anyone. That is the difference, and it is the reason I built it in.

What I believe about this

  • The week is the hard part, not the meeting. A meeting is one day. The app is for the six days in between.
  • Shame is not the medicine. Nothing in it is designed to make anyone feel worse about what they already know.
  • Privacy over community. There is an app lock and a discreet mode so nothing on a phone announces what it is.
  • Free has to mean free. No card to start, no time limit, and the SOS tools are never behind a paywall.
  • It is not a clinic. It does not diagnose or treat anything, and it says so plainly.

What I am looking for

People and organisations who are already sitting with this — treatment centres, recovery programs, schools, counsellors and the family-support groups around them — who want something their people can use on the nights between sessions. It is free, it needs no account, and there is nothing to sell anyone.
