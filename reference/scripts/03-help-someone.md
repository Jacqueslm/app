# How do you help someone who doesn't want help?

**~10 min · unrestricted · you are on this side right now**

The rule the whole app runs on applies double here: never blame the person
struggling to comfort the supporter, and never blame the supporter to comfort
the person struggling. Nothing in this script tells anyone to monitor, check,
search or confront. No pronouns for the person they love.

---

## COLD OPEN

You can't.

I'll say that at the start rather than make you sit through nine minutes
waiting for it. You cannot make somebody stop. There is no sentence. There's
no way of saying it, no article to send, no moment to pick.

If you're still here after that, it's because you already knew. You've been
trying for a while.

So this isn't about how to make them. It's about what's actually left, which
turns out to be more than nothing.

## WHY THE SENTENCE DOESN'T EXIST

People think there's a thing they haven't said yet. The right words, at the
right moment, and it'll land.

I've been on both sides of this and I can tell you it's not a language
problem. They already know. They knew before you did. Whatever you're working
up the courage to say, they've said worse to themselves at three in the
morning.

That's why it doesn't land. You're not delivering information. There's no
information missing.

## THE THING THAT ACTUALLY CHANGES

A boundary is a sentence about **what you will do**.

"I'm not having this conversation after ten at night."
"I'm not lending money again."
"I'm not covering for you at your mum's."

That's a boundary. It doesn't need their agreement. It doesn't need them to
understand it, or think it's fair, or stop being annoyed about it. You can
keep it entirely on your own.

A rule is a sentence about what *they* will do. "You need to stop by Friday."
And a rule needs them to cooperate, which means it isn't yours, which means
it'll break — and when it breaks you'll feel like you failed at something you
were never holding.

Every ounce of your effort that's gone into rules has gone nowhere. That's not
because you did it badly. It's because it was never available.

## THE CHECKING

Now the bit that's harder to say.

A lot of us end up checking. Pockets, phones, receipts, bins, the search
history, the app that shows where somebody is. And I want to be careful here
because I'm not telling you off — I understand exactly why. You're trying to
get one solid fact in a situation where nothing's solid.

But here's what's true. Finding it has never once changed the outcome. Not
once. You already knew before you looked. What the looking does is turn you
into somebody who searches their own house, and that costs you something every
time.

If you want a test: after you check, are you calmer or worse? Everyone I've
asked says worse.

## YOU'RE ALLOWED TO BE FURIOUS

Something nobody says to people in your position.

You can love somebody and be absolutely furious with them at the same time.
Those aren't opposites and being angry doesn't make you a hypocrite, or
unsupportive, or part of the problem.

The anger is usually accurate. You gave up years. You cancelled things. You
managed the temperature of a whole house so nothing would go off. Nobody
thanked you for any of it because nobody saw it.

What I'd say is: let some of it out on purpose, on an ordinary day, to
somebody safe. Because it comes out either way, and if it isn't on purpose it
comes out sideways, usually at somebody who didn't earn it.

## YOUR LIFE ISN'T THE THING TO PAUSE

The mistake I made, and I made it for a long time, is treating my own life as
something to restart afterwards. Once this is sorted. Once they're better.

This doesn't have a date on it. There's no afterwards you can schedule.

So the friend you stopped replying to — reply. The thing you used to do on a
Saturday — put it back in. Not as a reward for coping. As the thing that makes
coping possible for another year.

You're not going to help anybody from empty. That's not a slogan, it's
arithmetic.

## THE ONE THING TO ASK FOR

If you take one thing from this: get one person who knows the truth of your
week. Not a group, not a programme necessarily. One person.

And ask them for something specific. "Let me know if you need anything" is
unusable — nobody's ever acted on it. "Can you ring me Thursday at eight" is
usable. People are very good at specific and hopeless at general.

## CLOSE

You can't make them stop. That was true before you clicked on this.

What's yours: what you'll do, what you'll no longer do, and whether there's
one person who knows how you actually are.

That's not a consolation prize. In every version of this I've watched, it's
the part that decides how the next year goes for you — and, more often than
people expect, it's what changes the room enough that something else becomes
possible.
