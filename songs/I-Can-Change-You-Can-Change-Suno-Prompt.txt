SONG — "I CAN CHANGE, YOU CAN CHANGE"
Two tracks, one song. World peace, nations united, the powers dismantled.

The song is built as two generations. Part one is the wound and the machine;
part two is the waking up and the joining. They are written to the same key so
they cut together at the switch. Generate each block on its own, then join them.

HOW THE SWITCH WORKS
- Part one is 92 BPM, A minor, hip hop through four genre lanes.
- Part two is 104 BPM, A minor, Afrobeat and Latin trap through four more.
- Part one ends on a four-bar breakdown: kick, bass and one spoken line only,
  no vocal riding over it, so the join has somewhere to land.
- Part two opens cold on the log drum with no intro talk, so the switch is a
  door opening rather than a fade.
- Same five singers on every chorus, so the two halves are one song and not two.

THE CAST
Singers, five on every chorus, stacked in three-part harmony:
1. Alto lead — carries the hook line, warm and forward.
2. Tenor one — harmony above the lead, answers the second half of each line.
3. Tenor two — doubles the lead an octave down on the last line.
4. Soprano — top harmony, comes in on "hold hands with love for humanity".
5. Baritone — bottom harmony and the answered ad-libs. Also speaks the intro.

Rappers, eight, one sixteen-bar verse each, no two alike:
1. Street reporter — clipped, factual, angry but controlled.
2. Southern drawl — slow, wounded, trap-soul, the confessor.
3. West Coast — laid back, funk bass, the one who has seen people help people.
4. East London — double-time, the one who names the machine out loud.
5. Afrobeats melodist — half-sung, the one who brings the neighbours.
6. Latin trap — Spanish and English, the one who turns the wall into a dance.
7. Spoken word — quiet, the confession, the one who says "I've been a fool".
8. Gospel closer — the amen, the choir, the one who ends it in light.

Each verse is exactly sixteen bars. The chorus appears eight times: four in part
one, four in part two. That is the eight choruses and the sixteen-bar verses.

WHAT THE SONG ARGUES, IN ORDER
Part one: the border, the flag, the news, the bank, the prison, the pill, and
the TV are the tools, and the tool has no colour. Division is the product;
sedation is the delivery method. The enemy named in the song is greed and the
people who profit from it, never a race, a religion or a nation.
Part two: the drum, the table, the stranger's hand and the choir are the way
out. Unite as one, hold hands with love for humanity, dismantle by outgrowing
the thing rather than by hating a person.

LENGTH
Part one runs about three minutes. Part two runs about three minutes twenty.
Played as one song, that is a six-minute two-part record: part one on the first
half, part two after the switch, both choruses the same five voices.
