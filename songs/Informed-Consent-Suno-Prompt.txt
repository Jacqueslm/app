SUNO PROMPT — ORIGINAL HBCU MARCHING-BAND "INFORMED CONSENT" (EXTENDED CUT)
A fictional hearing-room debate on medical racism, consent and accountability.
Extended cut: a second round on vaccine-injury compensation and informed consent,
plus a maternal-mortality verse for Nia.
Two separate prompts: Style box, then Lyrics box.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
STYLE OF MUSIC (paste into the Style box - 789 characters)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
HBCU-style marching-band protest rap at 94 BPM in G minor. Live snare-line cadence, sousaphone bass on the downbeat, quad drums, brass stabs, cymbal crashes, stomp-and-clap field chant, one gavel hit. Male and female rappers trade hard, conversational bars; a cold, bureaucratic WHO representative answers in a flat, measured tone. The band chants the hook and punctuates each verse; no trap, no auto-tune, no EDM. Tight stadium room, close vocals, brass and drums forward, a stripped cadence under the bridge, full band on the last hook. The march is press-forward, not a bounce: kick on the floor, snares on the backbeat, sousaphone under the kick, and a brass answer after every rapper's turn. Defiant, forensic, cinematic, live. Original lyrics, melody, vocal cadence and drum pattern.

LYRICS (paste into the Lyrics box - 3986 characters)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
[Intro — drumline cadence, one gavel strike]
KADEEM: State your name for the record.
DR. MERCER: Dr. Mercer. World Health Organization.
NIA: Round two. We brought receipts.

[Chorus — Kadeem, Nia, band chant]
Informed consent
Show me the whole file
Show me the harm
Show me who profits
Then ask me again

[Verse 1 — Kadeem]
You flew in from Geneva with a folder and a flag.
Tuskegee, 1932: forty years of lies in a government coat.
Henrietta's cells in a jar, a body turned into a patent.
Women sterilized and filed under "public health."
Don't start the story at the needle.
Start at the hand that held it.

[WHO Representative — Dr. Mercer]
Those are graves in our history. We don't deny them.
But vaccines are tested. The FDA approves. The CDC recommends.
Federal, state, local—every hand points to the next.
We don't design medicine to harm.

[Kadeem]
Then why does the harm keep the same address?

[Verse 2 — Nia]
You poisoned Flint and called it a pipe.
You put the plant in Cancer Alley and called it a job.
You closed the hospital and called it a budget.
You drew the redline and called it a map.
It's not a secret syringe.
It's a ZIP code.
Mind, body, soul—you poisoned all three,
then filed it under "health disparity."
You pledged war with a pen, not an army.

[WHO Representative — Dr. Mercer]
Those are housing, labor, environment. Not WHO.
We issue guidance. We don't run cities.

[Nia]
Right. Everybody's a messenger.
Nobody's the hand.

[Round Two — Kadeem]
You say the science is safe. I'm not saying it isn't.
"Trust me" is not a study.
Show me the trial, the adverse events,
the contract, the price.
If the injury is rare, why is there a compensation court?
Why does the table pick which harm counts?
Why does the family carry the burden?

[WHO Representative — Dr. Mercer]
Compensation exists because harm exists.
No system is perfect. The table is reviewed.
We can't adjudicate every case at WHO.

[Nia]
Then perfect it. Fund it. Publish it.
Stop making the injured carry the whole case.

[Kadeem]
Informed consent means I can say no
and still get care.

[Nia]
It means the form is in my language
and the door stays open.

[WHO Representative — Dr. Mercer]
Consent forms are required.

[Kadeem]
A signature is not consent
if the only hospital for miles is closing.

[Verse 3 — Nia]
Ask the mother who bled through the shift
while the monitor called and nobody came.
Ask the mother who drove two counties
because the ward closed in her own.
Ask the mother told "calm down"
while her pressure climbed past the chart.
Ask the mother who signed a form
she couldn't read, in a room she couldn't choose.
They call it maternal mortality.
I call it a door that closed.

[WHO Representative — Dr. Mercer]
Those are health systems. Not WHO.

[Nia]
You issue guidance.
We issue obituaries.

[Kadeem]
You want to call our vaccines lethal?
I'll call your record lethal. There's a difference.
You want a lethal injection?
Try a lethal ZIP code.

[WHO Representative — Dr. Mercer]
That's not the same.

[Kadeem]
You're right. It's legal.

[Bridge — band drops to sousaphone and snare]
DR. MERCER: What do you want from me?
KADEEM: A receipt.
NIA: An apology with a budget.
KADEEM: A hospital that stays open.
NIA: A form that says "no" is allowed.
DR. MERCER: That's a lot.
KADEEM: It's the bare minimum.

[WHO Representative — Dr. Mercer]
You want me to say the science is a lie. I won't.
Vaccines save lives. That's real.

[Kadeem and Nia]
We didn't ask you to lie.
We asked you to account.

[Verdict — both rappers, band quiet]
The science is real.
The accountability is not.
The needle didn't kill my grandmother.
The closed hospital did.
The plant did.
The redline did.
The bill did.
The form did.
You didn't have to inject us.
You just had to forget us.
Case closed.

[Final Chorus — full band]
Informed consent
Show me the whole file
Show me the harm
Show me who profits

[Outro — gavel, drumline]
DR. MERCER: We're done here.
NIA: No. It's just getting started.
[Band hit]
